## 🗂️ Organizing Icons into Shaded Groups

Shaded groups are the heart of the setup. A group is a defined area on your desktop with a soft background and a title bar. To build one, pick a topic, drag the matching icons into the area, and adjust the size until everything fits comfortably. Leave a little breathing room around the icons so the group does not look cramped.

Think about the order of your groups as well as the order of icons inside them. Place the group you use most in the most convenient spot on screen. Put groups you check occasionally toward the edges. If one group becomes too full, that is a signal to split it into two groups or move some icons to a rolling page. Keeping groups modest in size makes them easier to scan and keeps the desktop from feeling dense again.

You can also use color or shading differences to distinguish categories. Slightly darker shading for work groups and lighter shading for entertainment groups gives your eye a quick cue. Consistency matters more than decoration: if work groups are always one style, you will recognize them instantly without reading the labels.

## 📄 Using Rolling Pages

Rolling pages give you more room without more clutter. Use them for categories that are real but not constantly needed, such as archived project shortcuts, seasonal tools, or a collection of utilities you use once in a while. Set a single rolling page area on the desktop and let it hold several pages of icons. Each page can have its own theme, so you always know what section you are browsing.

A practical pattern is one page for "everyday tools," one page for "project files," and one page for "occasional apps." The everyday page stays in view, and the others wait until you roll to them. Because pages occupy the same screen area, your desktop footprint does not grow as you add more icons. That is the main advantage over simply making more groups.

When you add a new icon, decide right away whether it belongs in a visible group or on a rolling page. Making that decision immediately prevents the slow accumulation of stray icons that undoes your organization over time.

## 🛠️ Everyday Maintenance

The best desktop layout is one you maintain with small habits. At the end of each week, spend a minute deleting shortcuts you no longer use. When a project ends, move its shortcuts to a rolling page or remove them. When a new tool becomes a daily habit, promote it to a main group.

Rename groups when their purpose changes so labels stay accurate. Resize groups when they grow or shrink. A layout that matches your current work is far more useful than one that matched your work six months ago. These small adjustments take seconds and keep the overall system trustworthy.

## 💡 Tips for a Cleaner Desktop

- Keep the number of visible groups small, ideally five or fewer.
- Use rolling pages for anything you open less than once a week.
- Name groups with one or two words.
- Leave empty space between groups so the desktop does not feel crowded.
- Group icons by when you use them, not only by what they are.
- Review your desktop once a week and remove unused shortcuts.
- Keep the most important group in the same place so it becomes muscle memory.
